# 🌐 Online Safety Act 2023

## 📌 Overview

The **Online Safety Act 2023** was introduced to make the internet safer for users, particularly children, by placing legal duties on online platforms to protect users from harmful content and online harms.

The Act builds on earlier legislation, including:

- Communications Act 2003
- Malicious Communications Act 1988
- Computer Misuse Act 1990

Unlike these earlier laws, the Online Safety Act focuses heavily on **platform accountability** rather than solely on individual offenders.

---

## ⚖️ Purpose of the Online Safety Act

The Act aims to:

- Improve online safety for users.
- Protect children from harmful content.
- Reduce the spread of illegal content.
- Increase accountability for technology companies.
- Strengthen privacy and user protections.
- Provide regulators with stronger enforcement powers.

---

## 🏛️ Key Areas Covered by the Act

| Area | What It Does | Who It Affects |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| **Duty of Care** | Requires platforms to identify and remove harmful content. | Social media companies, search engines, messaging services. |
| **Protection for Children** | Introduces stronger safeguards such as age verification and content controls. | Platforms hosting user-generated content. |
| **Illegal Content** | Requires the removal of illegal content including fraud, terrorism, and child exploitation material. | All regulated online services. |
| **User Empowerment Tools** | Gives users more control over the content they see and who can contact them. | UK internet users. |
| **Regulation by Ofcom** | Provides Ofcom with powers to investigate, fine, and sanction companies. | Ofcom and regulated organisations. |
| **Criminal Liability** | In certain circumstances, senior managers may face prosecution for serious failings. | Executives of regulated services. |

---

## 🚨 Key Offences and Penalties

### 1. Failure to Protect Users

Online platforms may face enforcement action if they fail to comply with their duty of care obligations.

Examples include:

- Failing to remove illegal content.
- Ignoring user-safety requirements.
- Failing to implement age verification.
- Failing to implement appropriate content controls.

**Potential penalties:**

- Fines of up to 10% of global annual turnover.
- Regulatory enforcement action.
- Potential criminal liability for senior managers in certain circumstances.

---

### 2. Harmful or Threatening Communications

Individuals may commit offences by sending:

- Threatening messages.
- Deliberately false communications intended to cause harm.
- Communications likely to cause serious emotional or psychological harm.

**Potential penalties:**

- Up to 2 years' imprisonment.
- Financial penalties.

---

### 3. Encouraging Self-Harm

It is an offence to encourage or assist self-harm through online communications.

Examples include:

- Posts
- Messages
- Videos
- Social media content

**Potential penalties:**

- Up to 5 years' imprisonment.

---

### 4. Sharing Intimate Images Without Consent

The Act strengthens protections against the non-consensual sharing of intimate material.

This includes:

- Sharing intimate images.
- Threatening to share intimate images.
- Creating sexual deepfakes.
- Manipulating images without consent.

**Potential penalties:**

- Imprisonment ranging from several months to multiple years depending on the circumstances.

---

### 5. Obstructing Ofcom Investigations

Companies and individuals must cooperate with Ofcom investigations.

Offences may include:

- Refusing to provide information.
- Providing false information.
- Deliberately obstructing investigations.

**Potential penalties:**

- Financial penalties.
- Potential criminal sanctions.

## 🌐 Impact on Internet Users in the UK

### ✅ Safer Social Media Environments

Platforms must act more quickly against:

- Abuse and harassment
- Fraud and scams
- Terrorist content
- Child exploitation material

**Impact:** Reduced exposure to harmful content and improved reporting systems.

---

### ✅ Stronger Protection for Children

Platforms are expected to implement:

- Age verification
- Parental controls
- Child safety settings
- Content filters

**Impact:** Improved protection for young users online.

---

### ✅ Greater User Control

Users may be provided with tools that allow them to:

- Block content types
- Restrict interactions
- Filter anonymous accounts
- Control online experiences

**Impact:** More personalised and safer user experiences.

---

### ✅ Improved Reporting Systems

Platforms must provide:

- Clear reporting mechanisms
- Transparent complaints processes
- Faster responses to reports

**Impact:** Easier reporting of harmful content and improved platform accountability.

---

### ✅ Reduced Online Fraud

Platforms are expected to combat:

- Fake advertisements
- Investment scams
- Impersonation scams
- Malicious links

**Impact:** Greater protection against online fraud.

---

### ✅ Stronger Protection Against Online Abuse

New offences help address:

- Threatening communications
- Self-harm encouragement
- Non-consensual image sharing
- Deepfake abuse

**Impact:** Stronger legal protection for users.

---

### ✅ Increased Transparency

Platforms may need to publish:

- Risk assessments
- Safety reports
- Information about harmful content
- Details of safety controls

**Impact:** Users can better understand how platforms manage online safety.

---

## 🛡️ Cyber Security Relevance

The Online Safety Act is relevant to cyber security because it:

- Supports protection against online threats.
- Encourages secure platform design.
- Promotes risk management practices.
- Strengthens incident reporting and response.
- Reduces opportunities for online fraud and abuse.
- Introduces governance obligations for technology companies.
