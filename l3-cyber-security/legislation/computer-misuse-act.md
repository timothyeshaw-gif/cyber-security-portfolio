# ⚖️ Computer Misuse Act 1990 (CMA 1990)

## 📌 Overview

The **Computer Misuse Act 1990 (CMA 1990)** is a UK law introduced to combat computer-related crime. It makes unauthorised access to computer systems and data a criminal offence and provides the legal framework for prosecuting hacking and other cyber crimes.

The Act was one of the first pieces of legislation specifically designed to address offences involving computers and remains a cornerstone of UK cyber-crime law.

---

## 🏛️ Main Offences Under the Act

| Section | Offence | Example |
|----------|----------|----------|
| **Section 1** | Unauthorised access to computer material | Logging into another person's account without permission. |
| **Section 2** | Unauthorised access with intent to commit or facilitate further offences | Accessing a system to steal money, personal data, or confidential information. |
| **Section 3** | Unauthorised acts with intent to impair operation of a computer | Deploying malware, ransomware, or carrying out denial-of-service attacks. |
| **Section 3ZA** | Unauthorised acts causing serious damage | Attacks that threaten national security, public safety, or critical infrastructure. |
| **Section 3A** | Making, supplying, or obtaining articles for use in offences | Creating, selling, sharing, or possessing hacking tools or malicious software. |

---

## ⚠️ Consequences of Committing These Offences

Penalties under the Computer Misuse Act can be severe and depend on the seriousness of the offence.

### Criminal Penalties

- **Section 1:** Up to **2 years imprisonment** and/or a fine.
- **Section 2:** Up to **5 years imprisonment** and/or a fine.
- **Section 3:** Up to **10 years imprisonment** and/or a fine.
- **Section 3ZA:** Up to **life imprisonment** where attacks cause serious harm to national security, human welfare, or critical infrastructure.

### Additional Consequences

- Permanent criminal record.
- Difficulty obtaining employment in IT, cyber security, finance, government, or regulated industries.
- Loss of professional credibility.
- Potential civil claims from victims seeking compensation for damages.

## 🔒 Why the Computer Misuse Act Matters

The Computer Misuse Act was the first UK law to specifically define and criminalise computer misuse.

It provides legal protection against:

- Hacking
- Malware attacks
- Ransomware attacks
- Unauthorised access to data
- Denial-of-service attacks
- Misuse of hacking tools

The Act helps protect:

- Individuals
- Businesses
- Government organisations
- Critical national infrastructure

---

## 🛡️ Cyber Security Relevance

The Computer Misuse Act is highly relevant to cyber security professionals because it establishes the legal boundaries of acceptable activity.

Activities such as:

- Penetration testing
- Vulnerability scanning
- Security assessments

must always be conducted with **explicit permission** from the owner of the system.

Without authorisation, these activities could potentially fall within the scope of CMA offences.

## 📝 Key Learning Points

Through studying the Computer Misuse Act 1990 I have learned that:

- Accessing systems without permission is a criminal offence.
- Intent plays an important role in determining the severity of offences.
- Cyber security professionals must always obtain authorisation before testing systems.
- The law applies to both technical attacks and the creation or distribution of malicious tools.
- The Act continues to play a vital role in modern cyber-crime investigations.
