# ⚖️ Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984 (PACE)

## 📌 Overview

The **Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984 (PACE)** is a UK law that provides the framework for police powers and safeguards when investigating criminal offences.

PACE establishes rules covering:

- Search and seizure
- Arrest and detention
- Interviewing suspects
- Collection of evidence
- Preservation of evidence

Although originally created before the widespread use of computers, PACE remains highly relevant to cyber crime investigations because it governs how digital evidence must be collected, handled, and presented in court.

---

## 🎯 Purpose of PACE

The Act was introduced to:

- Protect the rights of individuals.
- Ensure investigations are conducted fairly and lawfully.
- Prevent abuse of police powers.
- Establish clear procedures for gathering evidence.
- Improve the reliability and admissibility of evidence in court.

PACE aims to balance effective law enforcement with individual rights and freedoms.

---

## 🏛️ Key Areas Covered by PACE

### Search and Seizure

PACE allows authorised investigators to:

- Search premises under specific legal powers.
- Seize items that may be relevant to an investigation.
- Secure evidence that may otherwise be destroyed or altered.

In cyber investigations this may include:

- Computers
- Mobile devices
- Servers
- Hard drives
- USB storage devices
- Network equipment

---

### Arrest and Detention

PACE establishes legal requirements for:

- Arrest procedures
- Detention periods
- Access to legal representation
- Rights of suspects

These protections help ensure investigations are carried out fairly.

---

### Interviews and Statements

The Act defines procedures for:

- Suspect interviews
- Witness statements
- Recording evidence
- Use of interview transcripts

Evidence gathered improperly may be challenged in court.

---

## 💻 Cyber Security and Digital Forensics Relevance

PACE is particularly important in cyber security and digital forensics because digital evidence must be collected in a manner that maintains its integrity.

Examples of digital evidence include:

- Log files
- Emails
- Network traffic captures
- Hard drive images
- Mobile phone data
- Cloud service records

Investigators must be able to demonstrate that evidence has not been altered, damaged, or contaminated.

---

## 🔒 Chain of Custody

A key concept supported by PACE is the **chain of custody**.

This provides a documented record showing:

- Who collected the evidence.
- When it was collected.
- Where it was stored.
- Who accessed it.
- How it was transferred.

Maintaining a clear chain of custody helps ensure evidence remains admissible in court.

---

## 📝 Key Requirements for Digital Investigations

When handling digital evidence investigators should:

- Preserve original evidence wherever possible.
- Create forensic copies rather than working on originals.
- Document every action taken.
- Follow approved forensic procedures.
- Maintain audit trails.
- Ensure evidence remains secure.

These practices help maintain evidential integrity.

---

## ⚠️ Consequences of Failing to Follow PACE

Failure to follow PACE procedures may result in:

- Evidence being ruled inadmissible in court.
- Criminal cases collapsing.
- Challenges to the integrity of an investigation.
- Miscarriages of justice.
- Legal and disciplinary consequences for investigators.

---

## 🛡️ Why PACE Matters to Cyber Security

PACE plays an important role in cyber security investigations because:

- Digital evidence must meet legal standards.
- Organisations may need to support law enforcement investigations.
- Incident response activities often involve preserving evidence.
- Forensic investigations require proper evidence handling procedures.

It helps ensure evidence remains reliable and legally defensible.
