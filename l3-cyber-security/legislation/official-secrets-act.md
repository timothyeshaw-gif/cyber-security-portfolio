# 🏛️ Official Secrets Act 1989

## 📌 Overview

The **Official Secrets Act 1989 (OSA 1989)** is a UK law designed to protect sensitive government information relating to national security, defence, intelligence, and international relations.

The Act makes it a criminal offence for current and former government employees, members of the armed forces, intelligence personnel, contractors, and others entrusted with official information to disclose protected information without lawful authority.

Unlike the Official Secrets Act 1911, the 1989 Act focuses on specific categories of protected information rather than requiring proof that disclosure was damaging to national security.

---

## 🎯 Purpose of the Official Secrets Act

The Act was introduced to:

- Protect national security information.
- Prevent unauthorised disclosure of government secrets.
- Safeguard intelligence operations and sources.
- Protect defence-related information.
- Maintain trust in public services and government organisations.
- Support the security of the United Kingdom.

---

## 🏛️ Who the Act Applies To

The legislation applies to individuals who have access to official information through their employment or role.

Examples include:

- Civil servants
- Members of the armed forces
- Police officers
- Intelligence service employees
- Government contractors
- Individuals holding security clearances
- Former government employees with access to sensitive information

---

## 🔐 Protected Categories of Information

The Official Secrets Act protects several categories of sensitive information.

| Category | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Security and Intelligence** | Information relating to MI5, MI6, GCHQ, and intelligence operations. |
| **Defence** | Information relating to the armed forces, military capabilities, and defence operations. |
| **International Relations** | Information that could affect the UK's relationship with other countries or international organisations. |
| **Crime and Special Investigations** | Information relating to ongoing investigations or law enforcement activity. |
| **Information Obtained in Confidence** | Information received by government from another country or organisation in confidence. |
