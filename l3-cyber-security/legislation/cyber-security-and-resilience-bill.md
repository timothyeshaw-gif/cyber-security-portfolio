# 🛡️ Cyber Security and Resilience Bill (UK)

## 📌 Overview

The **Cyber Security and Resilience Bill** is expected to be introduced to the UK Parliament in late 2026, with implementation likely from 2027 onwards as part of the UK's wider cyber security strategy.

The Bill aims to strengthen the United Kingdom's cyber resilience by introducing stronger security requirements for public bodies, critical infrastructure operators, and organisations that provide essential services.

The legislation is intended to modernise cyber security regulation and address emerging threats to the UK's digital infrastructure.

---

## 🎯 Purpose of the Bill

The Cyber Security and Resilience Bill aims to:

- Strengthen the UK's cyber defence capabilities.
- Improve resilience against cyber attacks.
- Require higher cyber security standards across critical sectors.
- Improve incident reporting processes.
- Promote information sharing between government and industry.
- Support national cyber security objectives.
- Strengthen regulatory oversight and compliance.

---

## 🏛️ Key Objectives

The proposed legislation is expected to:

### Improve National Cyber Resilience

Organisations that provide essential services will be expected to demonstrate stronger cyber security controls and resilience measures.

### Strengthen Security Standards

Public bodies and operators of critical infrastructure may be required to implement more robust security measures to reduce cyber risk.

### Enhance Incident Reporting

Organisations will be expected to report significant cyber incidents more consistently and within defined reporting timeframes.

### Support Cooperation

The Bill aims to improve information sharing between:

- Government departments
- Regulators
- Critical infrastructure providers
- Cyber security organisations

### Strengthen Oversight

The legislation is expected to increase the role of national cyber security regulators in monitoring compliance and enforcing standards.

---

## 🔐 Expected Requirements

Organisations covered by the Bill may be required to:

- Identify and manage cyber risks.
- Implement appropriate security controls.
- Maintain cyber resilience plans.
- Conduct regular risk assessments.
- Report significant cyber incidents.
- Cooperate with relevant authorities.
- Demonstrate compliance with cyber security requirements.

---

## 🏢 Organisations Likely to be Affected

The legislation is expected to focus on organisations that provide services critical to society and the economy, including:

- Government departments
- Public sector organisations
- Energy providers
- Water companies
- Transport operators
- Healthcare organisations
- Digital infrastructure providers
- Telecommunications providers

## 🚨 Why the Bill is Important

The increasing number of cyber attacks against public and private organisations has highlighted the need for stronger cyber security requirements.

The Bill is expected to:

- Improve national resilience.
- Reduce cyber risks.
- Strengthen critical infrastructure protection.
- Improve incident response capability.
- Support national security objectives.

---

## 🛡️ Cyber Security Relevance

The Cyber Security and Resilience Bill reflects a growing shift from simply protecting systems to ensuring organisations can continue operating during and after cyber incidents.

The Bill promotes:

- Risk management
- Security governance
- Incident response planning
- Business continuity
- Supply chain security
- Organisational resilience
