# Portfolio Reflection: AI, Security Testing and Critical Thinking

## Overview

Today's learning took me beyond the technical aspects of cyber security and reinforced the importance of critical thinking, governance and risk management.

The session focused on different security testing methodologies and practices, including black box, white box and grey box testing, as well as the distinct roles performed by Red, Blue and Purple Teams. We also explored the limitations of automated vulnerability scanners and the importance of manual verification when assessing potential security weaknesses.

---

## Key Learning Points

### Automated Tools Are Not the Final Answer

One of the most valuable lessons from the session was understanding that security tools should be viewed as aids rather than unquestionable sources of truth.

Automated scanners can identify potential indicators of vulnerabilities, but their findings still require validation and interpretation by a skilled analyst. False positives, contextual factors and risk prioritisation all require human judgement.

As the saying goes:

> Trust, but verify.

---

### Understanding Different Testing Approaches

We explored several common penetration testing methodologies:

| Testing Method | Description |
|---------------|-------------|
| **Black Box Testing** | Testing with little or no prior knowledge of the target environment. |
| **Grey Box Testing** | Testing with limited knowledge or partial access. |
| **White Box Testing** | Testing with full knowledge of systems, architecture and credentials. |

Understanding the strengths and limitations of each approach highlighted how testing can be tailored to simulate different real-world attack scenarios.

---

### Red, Blue and Purple Teams

A particularly interesting area was understanding the relationship between Red, Blue and Purple Teams.

- **Red Teams** adopt an attacker's mindset and attempt to identify weaknesses.
- **Blue Teams** focus on detection, monitoring and defence.
- **Purple Teams** bring both perspectives together to improve overall security effectiveness.

This collaborative approach demonstrates that security is not simply about finding vulnerabilities, but about continually improving organisational resilience.

---

## AI, Governance and Cyber Security

Outside the classroom, I watched a documentary covering AI, OpenAI, Elon Musk and the race towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

The documentary prompted me to research the subject further, exploring:

- AI safety concerns
- Governance challenges
- Legal disputes
- Infrastructure requirements
- Organisational accountability
- The safeguards needed for increasingly capable AI systems

Initially, AI governance and penetration testing appeared to be entirely separate topics. However, the more I researched, the clearer the similarities became.

Whether we are assessing security controls within an organisation or evaluating the risks associated with advanced AI systems, the underlying objectives remain remarkably similar:

- Understand risk
- Challenge assumptions
- Identify weaknesses
- Evaluate safeguards
- Improve resilience
- Ask difficult questions before somebody else does
