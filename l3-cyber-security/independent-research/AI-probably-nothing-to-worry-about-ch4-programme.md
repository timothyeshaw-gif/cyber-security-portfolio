# Portfolio Reflection: AI - Probably Nothing to Worry About (Channel 4)

**Date Viewed:** 12 November 2026  
**Broadcast Time:** 22:00 - 00:30  
**Channel:** Channel 4

## Overview

On 12 November 2026, I watched *AI - Probably Nothing to Worry About*, a documentary exploring the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence, the organisations driving its development, and the potential impact AI may have on society.

The programme featured leading figures in the AI industry, including Elon Musk, Demis Hassabis and Sam Altman, and examined the work being undertaken by Google DeepMind and OpenAI. It explored both the opportunities and concerns associated with increasingly powerful AI systems.

## Key Learning Points

### The Race for Advanced AI

One of the programme's central themes was the growing competition to develop increasingly capable AI systems. The documentary highlighted how access to world-class researchers and vast computing resources has become a strategic advantage in the pursuit of artificial intelligence.

The programme raised the question of whether organisations that achieve artificial superintelligence first could gain unprecedented influence over global economic, scientific and technological development.

### Lee Sedol vs AlphaGo

A particularly memorable segment revisited the match between Korean Go champion Lee Sedol and DeepMind's AlphaGo.

Go had long been considered a game requiring intuition, creativity and strategic thinking that computers would struggle to replicate. AlphaGo's victory demonstrated how machine learning systems can outperform even the most accomplished human experts in highly specialised domains.

What I found most striking was Lee Sedol's reflection following the match. His comments highlighted both the emotional and intellectual impact of witnessing AI surpass human capability in an area once considered uniquely human.

### Human-AI Symbiosis

The programme contrasted concerns about AI replacing people with Sam Altman's vision of human-AI symbiosis.

Rather than viewing AI as a competitor, this perspective sees AI as a tool that augments human intelligence, helping people make better decisions, solve problems more effectively and increase productivity.

### Democratisation of AI

Another important theme was the democratisation of AI.

While the development of advanced AI is concentrated within a small number of organisations, there is a growing debate about how the benefits of these technologies should be shared. The programme explored the balance between innovation, accessibility, safety and governance
