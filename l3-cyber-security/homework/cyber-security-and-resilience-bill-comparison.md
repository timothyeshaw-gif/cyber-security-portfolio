# 📝 Homework – Cyber Security and Resilience Bill vs NIS2 Directive

## 📌 Overview

The **Cyber Security and Resilience Bill** is expected to be introduced to the UK Parliament by late 2026, with implementation likely from 2027 onwards as part of the UK's wider cyber security strategy.

The proposed legislation aims to strengthen the UK's cyber resilience, improve protection against cyber attacks, and increase accountability for organisations that provide essential services.

---

## 🎯 Objectives of the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill

The proposed Bill aims to:

- Strengthen the UK's cyber defence and resilience against cyber attacks.
- Require public bodies and critical infrastructure operators to meet higher security standards.
- Improve incident reporting and information sharing between government and industry.
- Support the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) in monitoring and enforcing compliance.

---

## ⚖️ Comparison: Cyber Security and Resilience Bill vs NIS2 Directive

| Aspect | Cyber Security and Resilience Bill (UK) | NIS2 Directive (EU) |
|----------|----------|----------|
| **Scope** | Applies to UK organisations and public bodies. | Applies to EU member states and regulated organisations. |
| **Focus** | National resilience and government accountability. | Network and information system security across the EU. |
| **Regulator** | UK Government and National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC). | National authorities within each EU member state. |
| **Reporting** | Mandatory incident reporting for critical sectors. | Mandatory reporting for essential and important entities. |
| **Goal** | Strengthen the UK's cyber defence and response capability. | Harmonise cyber security standards across the European Union. |

---

## 🛡️ Similarities Between the Two Frameworks

Both the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill and the NIS2 Directive aim to:

- Improve cyber resilience.
- Strengthen security controls.
- Increase organisational accountability.
- Improve incident reporting processes.
- Protect critical infrastructure.
- Promote effective cyber risk management.

Both frameworks recognise that cyber security is essential to economic stability, public services, and national security.

---

## 🔍 Key Differences

Although the objectives are similar, there are some important differences.

### Cyber Security and Resilience Bill (UK)

The UK's proposed legislation focuses on:

- National cyber resilience.
- Government oversight.
- Critical infrastructure protection.
- Organisational accountability across public services.

### NIS2 Directive (EU)

The NIS2 Directive focuses on:

- Harmonising cyber security standards across member states.
- Strengthening private-sector cyber security.
- Creating a consistent security baseline throughout the EU.

---

## 📝 Key Learning Points

Through researching these two frameworks I have learned that:

- Modern cyber security legislation increasingly focuses on resilience as well as prevention.
- Governments are placing greater responsibility on organisations that provide important services.
- Incident reporting is becoming a critical component of cyber security governance.
- Organisations must be able to demonstrate compliance through risk management and security controls.
- National cyber security strategies increasingly rely on cooperation between government and industry.
