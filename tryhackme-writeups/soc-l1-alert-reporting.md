# TryHackMe - SOC Level 1: Alert Reporting

## Overview

This room focused on the process of documenting and reporting security alerts within a Security Operations Centre (SOC). The exercises demonstrated how analysts communicate investigation findings, record evidence, and provide actionable information to support effective incident management.

## Skills Demonstrated

- Security alert reporting
- Incident documentation
- Evidence collection
- Investigation summarisation
- Written communication
- Escalation procedures
- Security operations workflows
- Analyst reporting

## Key Concepts Covered

### Alert Documentation

Accurate documentation ensures investigations can be reviewed, validated and handed over to other analysts when necessary.

### Evidence Recording

Analysts must collect and preserve relevant evidence throughout an investigation to support findings and maintain an audit trail.

### Report Writing

Security reports should be clear, concise and structured to communicate key information to technical and non-technical stakeholders.

### Escalation Support

Well-written reports enable senior analysts and incident responders to make informed decisions quickly during active investigations.

## Investigation Process

The room demonstrated the importance of:

1. Reviewing alert details.
2. Investigating supporting evidence.
3. Determining whether activity is benign or malicious.
4. Recording findings accurately.
5. Identifying recommended next actions.
6. Escalating incidents where appropriate.

## Key Learning Outcomes

After completing this room I can:

- Document security investigations in a structured manner.
- Record relevant evidence and findings.
- Communicate investigation outcomes clearly.
- Produce concise and actionable security reports.
- Support incident response through effective documentation.
- Understand the importance of maintaining complete audit trails.

## Workplace Application

In a real-world SOC environment, these skills would be used to:

- Prepare investigation reports for security incidents.
- Document indicators of compromise (IOCs).
- Record analyst findings and recommendations.
- Support incident escalation processes.
- Maintain audit trails for compliance and forensic purposes.
- Improve communication between SOC teams and stakeholders.

## Reflection

This room reinforced the importance of communication and documentation within cyber security operations. While technical investigation skills are essential, accurate reporting is equally important because it enables teams to understand what happened, what evidence was found, and what actions should be taken. The room highlighted how effective reporting supports incident response, knowledge sharing and organisational learning.
