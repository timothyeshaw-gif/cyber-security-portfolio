# TryHackMe - SOC Level 1 Alert Triage

## Overview

This room introduced the responsibilities of a SOC (Security Operations Centre) Level 1 Analyst and the process of alert triage. The focus was on assessing security alerts, determining their severity, identifying false positives, and deciding when incidents should be escalated for further investigation.

## Objectives

- Understand the role of a SOC Level 1 Analyst.
- Learn the alert triage process.
- Differentiate between true positives and false positives.
- Assess alert severity and potential business impact.
- Understand escalation procedures and incident handling workflows.

## Key Concepts

### Alert Triage

Alert triage is the process of reviewing and prioritising security alerts to determine whether they represent a genuine security incident. Effective triage helps security teams focus their efforts on the most significant threats while reducing time spent investigating false positives.

### True Positives and False Positives

During triage, analysts must determine whether an alert represents genuine malicious activity or benign behaviour.

- **True Positive** – A genuine security threat requiring investigation or response.
- **False Positive** – An alert generated by legitimate activity that does not represent a threat.

### Prioritisation and Severity

Not all alerts carry the same level of risk. Analysts must consider:

- The criticality of the affected system.
- The likelihood of compromise.
- The potential business impact.
- The scope of the activity.
- Any indicators of compromise (IoCs).

This allows security teams to focus resources on the highest-priority incidents.

### Escalation

When an alert cannot be resolved at Tier 1 or appears to indicate malicious activity, it should be escalated to senior analysts or incident responders for further investigation.

Having clear escalation procedures helps ensure incidents are handled efficiently and consistently.

## Connection to Incident Response

This room links directly to the **Detection and Analysis** phase of the NIST Incident Response Lifecycle.

A SOC analyst may identify suspicious activity through security alerts, logs, or monitoring tools. These alerts are then triaged to determine whether they represent genuine incidents requiring activation of the Incident Response Plan.

Accurate triage helps organisations:

- Detect threats early.
- Reduce response times.
- Prioritise incidents effectively.
- Preserve evidence.
- Support incident response activities.

## Key Takeaways

- Not every security alert represents a real threat.
- Alert triage is essential for identifying genuine incidents.
- Analysts must balance technical evidence with business impact.
- Effective documentation supports investigations and audit trails.
- Escalation procedures are critical when incidents exceed the authority or capability of a Level 1 analyst.
- Alert triage forms an important part of the Detection and Analysis phase of incident response.
