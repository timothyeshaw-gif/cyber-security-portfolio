# Reflection - Introductory DFIR Module

## Why I Chose This Room

I selected this room because I wanted to build a stronger understanding of Digital Forensics and Incident Response (DFIR), an area that sits at the intersection of cyber security operations, investigation and evidence gathering.

As someone developing skills for SOC, GRC and incident management roles, understanding how organisations respond to incidents is essential.

## What I Learned

The module introduced the relationship between digital forensics and incident response.

Incident response focuses on identifying, containing and recovering from incidents, while digital forensics focuses on understanding what happened and preserving evidence.

I learned that responding to an incident is not simply about fixing the immediate problem. Organisations must also understand:

- How the incident occurred
- What systems were affected
- What evidence exists
- Whether the incident could happen again
- What improvements should be implemented

## Incident Response Lifecycle

One of the most useful concepts was the incident response lifecycle:

### Preparation

Organisations create policies, procedures, tools and training before incidents occur.

### Detection and Analysis

Potential incidents are identified through monitoring, alerts, user reports or security tools.

### Containment

Actions are taken to prevent the incident from spreading or causing further damage.

### Eradication

The root cause is removed from the environment.

### Recovery

Systems are restored to normal operation.

### Lessons Learned

A review is conducted to identify improvements and prevent recurrence.

## Connection to Post-Mortems

This module linked closely with my learning about incident post-mortems.

A post-mortem focuses on:

- What happened
- Why it happened
- The impact on users and the business
- The effectiveness of detection and response
- Root causes
- Lessons learned
- Future improvements

I particularly found the concept of blameless post-mortems interesting because the focus is on improving processes rather than assigning blame to individuals.

## Key Takeaway

One of my biggest takeaways from this room was that successful cyber security is not just about preventing incidents. It is equally important to be able to detect, investigate, document and learn from incidents when they occur.

Strong incident response and forensic processes enable organisations to recover more quickly, improve security controls and reduce the likelihood of similar incidents in the future.
